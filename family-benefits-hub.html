Advance Directive & Healthcare POA

Everything else on this site assumes you've passed. This section is different — it's about incapacity: a coma, stroke, or medical crisis where you're alive but can't speak for yourself. A regular power of attorney becomes void the moment you die, so it's no help after death — but it (and a healthcare-specific version) is exactly what your spouse needs while you're alive and unable to communicate.

Idaho's Advance Directive (two parts)

  • Durable Power of Attorney for Healthcare: names a Healthcare Agent (typically your spouse) who can make medical decisions and talk to your care team if you can't. Only takes effect once you're unable to communicate.
  • Living Will: states your wishes about life-sustaining treatment at the end of life — bundled with the Healthcare POA in Idaho's standard form.
  • Idaho also has a POST form (Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment) for anyone with a serious illness — actual medical orders signed by a physician/APRN/PA. If one exists, it takes precedence over and effectively updates the living will.

Idaho Healthcare Directive Registry

You can complete and store your Advance Directive online through Idaho's Healthcare Directive Registry — free, and once submitted it's immediately accessible to any Idaho provider who needs it, so your spouse doesn't have to produce a paper copy in an emergency.

Durable Financial / General Power of Attorney (a separate document)

A durable power of attorney for finances is different from the healthcare one — it lets your spouse handle bills, benefits paperwork, and accounts if you're incapacitated but alive. Like the healthcare POA, it becomes void at death (the will/executor takes over at that point). Worth having both, ideally drafted together with an attorney.

References: Idaho Dept. of Health & Welfare – Start Your Advance Directive · Idaho Healthcare Directive Registry

Military Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP)

SBP is a Department of Defense program, administered by DFAS (not the VA), that pays an eligible surviving spouse a monthly annuity for life, replacing a portion of the retired pay that stops when the retiree dies.

Key facts

  • A spouse named as beneficiary who was married to the service member on their date of retirement is eligible immediately, regardless of how long they were married.
  • A retiree who was unmarried at retirement can add Spouse SBP later; the election must be made within one year of the first marriage after retirement.
  • SBP annuities receive an annual COLA — a 2.8% increase took effect December 1, 2025.
  • The "widow's tax" offset has been eliminated — SBP and DIC can now be received in full at the same time (no more dollar-for-dollar reduction).

How to file / report a death

  • Contact DFAS to report the retiree's death using the online "Report a Retiree's Death" tool via AskDFAS, or call the DFAS Customer Care Center: 1-800-321-1080.
  • DFAS will need a certified copy of the death certificate and the retiree's SBP annuitant/claim information.
Official references: Military OneSource – About SBP · Military.com – SBP in 2026 · DFAS.mil

Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC)

DIC is a tax-free monthly benefit paid by the VA to eligible surviving spouses, children, or parents when a veteran's death was connected to military service, or when the veteran had a permanent, total (100%) service-connected disability rating for at least 10 years before death.

Key facts

  • 2026 base DIC rate for a surviving spouse: $1,699.36/month (effective Dec. 1, 2025, after a 2.8% COLA).
  • Surviving spouse eligibility generally requires marriage to the veteran for at least 1 year before death (or a child together), and continuous cohabitation unless separation wasn't the spouse's fault.
  • Dependent children may qualify if under 18, between 18–23 and in a VA-approved school program, or permanently incapable of self-support due to a disability that began before age 18.
  • Some low-income surviving parents may qualify for an income-based DIC benefit.
  • DIC and SBP can now be received in full simultaneously (the offset was repealed).

How to apply

  • Primary form: VA Form 21P-534EZ (covers DIC, Survivors Pension, and accrued benefits).
  • Apply online at VA.gov, by mail, in person at a VA regional office, or with help from a Veterans Service Officer (VSO) — VSO help is free.
Official references: VA.gov – About DIC · VA.gov – DIC Parent Rates

Your 100% P&T status & the DIC "10-year rule"

Since a veteran's death connected to service isn't the only path to DIC, a surviving spouse can also qualify if the veteran was rated totally disabled (100% P&T) for at least 10 years before death — or at least 5 years from the date of discharge, or at least 1 year if a former POW — even if the service-connected condition wasn't the actual cause of death. Track the date your P&T rating became effective below so it's easy to see whether the 10-year (or 5-year) threshold has been met.

Other VA Survivor Benefits

VA Caregiver Support (PCAFC) — a current benefit, not a survivor benefit

Everything else on this page is about what happens after your death. This one is different — if your spouse provides regular hands-on care for you, this VA program supports her directly, right now.

  • Eligibility: you need a combined service-connected disability rating of 70% or higher (your 100% P&T rating qualifies) and require at least 6 months of continuous in-person personal care.
  • Benefits for the caregiver: a monthly tax-free stipend (2026 figures run roughly $1,900–$3,800/month depending on location and level of care needed), respite care, mental health counseling, and beneficiary travel support.
  • Health insurance through CHAMPVA is available to the caregiver only if she isn't otherwise insured — since she's already covered under your TRICARE, this specific piece likely won't apply to her.
  • Apply with VA Form 10-10CG; a VA social worker completes an in-home assessment as part of the process.
Reference: VA Caregiver Support Program

Survivors Pension

A separate, tax-free, income-based monthly benefit for low-income, unremarried surviving spouses (and dependent children) of wartime veterans whose death was not service-connected. Filed on the same Form 21P-534EZ as DIC.

TRICARE (health coverage)

As a military retiree, your spouse is already TRICARE-eligible as your dependent, and stays eligible after your death.

  • When a retiree dies, the surviving spouse automatically remains TRICARE-eligible (Prime, Select, or TRICARE For Life, whichever applied) with no application needed and no coverage gap, as long as she doesn't remarry.
  • Remarriage rule is strict: if she remarries, TRICARE eligibility ends permanently at any age — unlike SBP/DIC, which have a "remarriage after 55 is protected" rule, TRICARE has no such protection unless she remarries another TRICARE-eligible retiree or service member.
  • Report the death in DEERS (Defense Enrollment Eligibility Reporting System) promptly — this is what keeps her TRICARE record accurate and claims processing without interruption. A funeral home or the DFAS/casualty assistance process can point her to the nearest ID card/DEERS office if a new ID card is needed.

Social Security Survivor Benefits

Separate from VA/DFAS — administered by the Social Security Administration, not the VA. Two distinct benefits:

  • A monthly survivor benefit for a surviving spouse, available starting at age 60 (age 50 if disabled), or at any age if caring for the deceased's child under 16 or disabled. The amount ranges from about 71.5% of the worker's benefit (claimed early) up to 100% at the survivor's full retirement age. Generally requires the marriage to have lasted at least 9 months (exceptions apply).
  • A one-time $255 lump-sum death payment to an eligible spouse or child — must be claimed within 2 years of death.
Call SSA directly to start either benefit: 1-800-772-1213.

Survivors' & Dependents' Educational Assistance (Chapter 35 / DEA)

Up to 36 months of education/training benefits for spouses and children of veterans who died from a service-connected condition, or who are permanently and totally disabled from one.

Burial & Memorial Benefits

  • Burial in a VA national cemetery at no cost (spouse/dependents may also be eligible).
  • Government-furnished headstone or marker.
  • U.S. burial flag.
  • Presidential Memorial Certificate.

Home Loan & Fee Waivers

Some surviving spouses (e.g., DIC recipients) may qualify for a VA home loan Certificate of Eligibility and funding fee exemption.

Start here: VA.gov – Family & Caregiver Benefits · Local help: your county Veterans Service Officer (VSO)

Federal (FERS) Retirement & Survivor Benefit

As a federal civilian employee (VA), you separately accrue a FERS (Federal Employees Retirement System) pension through OPM — this is distinct from military retired pay and SBP. FERS lets you elect a survivor annuity for your spouse, which is the "federal government survivor benefit at 62" referenced elsewhere on this site.

Survivor annuity election options

  • Full survivor benefit: spouse receives 50% of your unreduced annual basic annuity; your own annuity is reduced by 10% to pay for it.
  • Partial survivor benefit: spouse receives 25% of your unreduced annual basic annuity; your own annuity is reduced by 5%.
  • No survivor benefit: requires your spouse's notarized consent at the time you file for retirement.

Age 62 & deferred retirement

If you leave federal service before your Minimum Retirement Age with at least 5 years of creditable civilian service (and don't withdraw your retirement contributions), you're eligible for a deferred annuity starting at age 62, unreduced. Two things to plan around: deferred retirement doesn't carry FEHB (health insurance) or FEGLI (life insurance) into retirement, and it isn't eligible for the FERS Special Retirement Supplement. If instead you retire directly from federal service at/after your Minimum Retirement Age with enough years of service, different rules and supplement eligibility may apply — confirm your specific path with your HR benefits office or OPM before you separate.

FEGLI (Federal Employees' Group Life Insurance)

  • Basic: annual basic pay (rounded up to the next $1,000) plus $2,000.
  • Option A: flat additional $10,000.
  • Option B: up to 5 multiples of basic pay.
  • Option C (family): up to 5 multiples of $5,000 for a spouse and $2,500 per multiple for each eligible child.
  • At retirement you elect whether Option B/C coverage reduces over time; Option A can continue into retirement. FEGLI does not carry over if you separate via deferred retirement rather than retiring directly.
References: OPM.gov – Survivor Benefits FAQ · OPM.gov – FERS Types of Retirement · OPM.gov – FEGLI

Life Insurance & TSP

SGLI / VGLI (life insurance)

  • SGLI (Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance) pays out to the beneficiaries the service member designated while on active duty.
  • VGLI (Veterans' Group Life Insurance) is the renewable term policy a veteran converts SGLI into after leaving service — coverage on file: $400,000. VGLI is available in $10,000 increments up to a $500,000 maximum.
  • Veterans have 485 days (1 year + 120 days) from separation/retirement to convert SGLI to VGLI; the first 240 days of that window is "guaranteed issue" — no medical exam required. After 240 days, proof of good health is required to enroll.
  • Free VA Beneficiary Financial Counseling and online will-preparation services are available to SGLI/VGLI/TSGLI beneficiaries.
  • When an active-duty service member dies, a Casualty Assistance Officer (CAO) is normally assigned within 24 hours to help the family file the SGLI/VGLI claim and coordinate other benefits.

TSP (Thrift Savings Plan)

  • If a designated beneficiary is on file, funds go directly to them.
  • If no beneficiary is designated, TSP pays out by statutory order of precedence: spouse → children (equally) → parents → estate → next of kin.
  • Check/update TSP beneficiary designations regularly at tsp.gov.
References: Military OneSource – SGLI · VA.gov – VGLI · TSP.gov

Idaho Survivor Benefits

What Idaho offers your surviving spouse specifically — not your current veteran benefits, just what carries over or applies to her after your death.

Property tax reduction continues for your unremarried surviving spouse

  • Idaho law (Idaho Code §63-705A) lets an unremarried surviving spouse keep the disabled veteran property tax reduction you currently have — up to $1,500 off property tax — on the same home, for as long as she doesn't remarry, sell, or move.
  • It is not transferable to a different property if she moves.
  • She'll want to notify the county assessor of the death to keep the reduction applied correctly going forward.

State income tax on her survivor income

  • DIC is not taxed by Idaho (it's already federally tax-free).
  • Your SBP annuity to her: up to roughly $40,536 (indexed, recalculated yearly) can be deducted from Idaho taxable income under the "Retired Service Member" category of Idaho's Retirement Benefits Deduction, as long as she hasn't remarried and meets the same age/disability test that applied to you.
  • Your FERS survivor annuity to her will likely not qualify for this state deduction — Idaho's deduction for federal civilian pensions only covers CSRS/Foreign Service (FSRDS) pensions and specifically excludes FERS, regardless of who's receiving it. Worth confirming with a tax preparer.
References: Idaho Code §63-705A – Surviving Spouse Property Tax Reduction · Idaho State Tax Commission – Retirement Benefits Deduction · Idaho Division of Veterans Services

State rules and dollar amounts change yearly — verify current figures with the Idaho State Tax Commission or your county assessor before relying on them.
